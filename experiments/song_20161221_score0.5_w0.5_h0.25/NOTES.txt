Regarding, 2-D UMAP plots, which I doubt can have real utility in general, pointing Claude to Fig. 3 of
the TweetyBERT paper, it said:

    This is actually a much more legitimate use of 2-D UMAP than typical — a few key differences from your situation:

      1. Birdsong syllables are much more stereotyped than humpback calls. Canary syllables repeat thousands of times
         with very little variation, so the embedding manifold is genuinely low-dimensional (ring/loop shapes in panel B).
         The 2-D projection preserves real structure because the intrinsic dimensionality is already low.
      2. The coloring is the point — they're not using the 2-D plot to discover clusters, they're using it to
         validate that ground-truth syllable labels (colors) align with embedding geometry. That's a legitimate qualitative check.
      3. Panel C is the honest comparison — showing that raw spectrogram UMAP (without TweetyBERT) produces blobs with
         no separable structure, while panel B shows clean rings per syllable type. The contrast makes the point.

      For humpback song the analogy breaks down because:
      - Call types are far more variable and context-dependent
      - The intrinsic dimensionality is likely much higher
      - 1 million time bins of highly stereotyped canary syllables is a very different regime from 40k windows of complex whale song

      So the TweetyBERT authors are using 2-D UMAP appropriately for their domain. It doesn't generalize as a methodology to yours.
